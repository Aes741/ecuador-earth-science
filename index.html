A small country with varied landscapes

Four regions, many connections

Ecuador lies on the Pacific coast of northwestern South America. Its official tourism guide presents four regions: Galápagos, the Coast, the Andes, and Amazonia. These neighboring worlds create a compelling setting for studying landforms, water, climate, and ecosystems.

The country’s physical geography is closely connected to its people and history. Quito, the capital, was built on the site of an Inca city and sits high in the Andes. Its historic center reflects layers of Indigenous and colonial history and is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site.

A place of global significance1978Quito and the Galápagos Islands were among the first properties inscribed on UNESCO’s World Heritage List.

History, culture & livelihoods

A landscape that shapes daily life

History in the highlands

Quito’s historic center preserves architecture and urban history spanning Indigenous and colonial periods. The city was inscribed by UNESCO in 1978.

Cultural diversity

Ecuador’s cultural life reflects Indigenous, Spanish, and Afro-descendant influences, alongside the distinct identities of its regions and communities.

Natural resources

Agriculture, oil, minerals, and hydropower are among Ecuador’s resources. The World Bank also notes climate and fiscal pressures affecting the country.
