Where is Ecuador?

A country crossed by the Equator

Ecuador is in northwestern South America on the Pacific Ocean. It borders Colombia to the north and Peru to the east and south; the Galápagos Islands are about 1,000 km west of the mainland.

The mainland extends approximately from 1°28′ N to 5°01′ S and from 75°11′ W to 81°01′ W. Including the Galápagos, Ecuador’s territory reaches about 92° W. Its name comes from the Equator, which crosses the northern part of the country.

Country history

When has Ecuador existed?

Before the republic

Peoples including the Quitu, Cañari, and others lived in the region before Inca expansion. Spain established the Audiencia of Quito in 1563.

Independence

Quito’s independence movement began in 1809. The territory became independent from Spain through the wider liberation campaign and joined Gran Colombia in 1822.

Modern Ecuador

Ecuador separated from Gran Colombia and became a republic in 1830. The modern state has continued through constitutional changes and negotiated boundary adjustments.

People who mapped the region

Pedro Vicente Maldonado

Pedro Vicente Maldonado (1704–1748) was an Ecuadorian scientist, geographer, and cartographer from Riobamba. He worked with Charles-Marie de La Condamine during the French Geodesic Mission, which measured an arc of the meridian near the Equator to help test Earth’s shape.

Maldonado surveyed routes between Quito and the Pacific, recorded rivers and settlements, and produced a detailed map of the Real Audiencia de Quito. His work connected local geographic knowledge with European surveying and made the region more legible to later travelers and administrators.
